# Why I Fully Embraced Vibe Coding (And Why Quality Doesn’t Have to Suffer)

I’ve made a permanent shift in how I work.

I’m now fully embracing vibe coding, using AI to write the majority of the code while I stay completely in charge of the thinking, the architecture, the edge cases, and the final quality. I no longer treat writing every line by hand as a badge of honor. I treat it as an unnecessary bottleneck.

### The Old Way Was Too Slow

For a long time I insisted on typing every single character myself. It felt disciplined. It felt pure. But the reality was simple: it was slow. Painfully slow.

Every new feature required grinding through boilerplate, looking up syntax, rewriting the same patterns, and fixing minor mistakes that an AI could have avoided in seconds. Rapid development was almost impossible. Ideas would sit in my head for days or weeks because the cost of turning them into working code was just too high. I was spending more energy on the mechanical act of writing code than on the actual hard part, deciding what should be built and why.

That friction was no longer acceptable.

### The New Way: AI Writes, I Think

Now the division of labor is clear:

- I own the thinking, the product decisions, the system design, and the quality bar.
- AI handles the first draft of the code, the repetitive parts, the syntax, and the initial structure.

I still review every important line. I still rewrite what doesn’t meet my standards. I still reject outputs that feel wrong. The AI is a very fast junior developer who never gets tired, not a replacement for judgment.

The speed difference is dramatic. Features that used to take a full day can now be prototyped in an afternoon. Experiments that I previously abandoned because they were “too much work to try” are now easy to test. The feedback loop got dramatically shorter, and that changes everything.

### This Approach Extends Beyond Code

I’m applying the same principle to writing and research. AI helps me draft blog posts, explore topics, and organize information. But the core ideas, the opinions, the structure, and the final voice remain mine. This post is a good example, the thoughts are mine; the AI simply helped me get them onto the page more efficiently.

I’m not outsourcing my thinking. I’m removing the tedious parts so I can spend more time on the parts that actually matter.

### Pros Clearly Outweigh the Cons

Of course there are trade-offs. AI-generated code can sometimes be verbose, slightly off-style, or miss subtle context. It can introduce patterns you wouldn’t have chosen yourself. You still need to review carefully.

But the advantages are far larger:

- Speed: You can explore more ideas in the same amount of time.
- Reduced friction: Less time fighting syntax and boilerplate means more mental energy for architecture and product decisions.
- Higher experimentation rate: When the cost of trying something drops, you try more things. Some of them turn out to be good.
- Consistency on routine work: AI is surprisingly good at the boring, repetitive parts that humans often rush or mess up when tired.
- Scalability of output: One person can now produce what used to require a small team of pure typing effort.

The cons are manageable with good habits. The pros compound over time.

### Why Quality Doesn’t Have to Drop

The biggest fear people have is that quality will suffer. I don’t believe it has to — if you keep the right mindset.

Quality is not determined by who typed the characters. Quality is determined by:

1. Clear thinking before any code is written
2. Strong review standards after the code is generated
3. Willingness to rewrite anything that feels off
4. Understanding the code well enough to own it

When you treat AI as a drafting tool rather than an authority, you stay in control. You still decide the architecture. You still catch the subtle bugs. You still enforce the style and performance requirements that matter for your project. In many cases the overall quality can even improve, because you now have more time and energy left for the high-leverage decisions instead of grinding through mechanical work.

Hand-writing every line used to feel like craftsmanship. Increasingly it just feels like friction.

Vibe coding, done properly, is not laziness. It’s leverage.
